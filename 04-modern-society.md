## The UK today

### Geography

- The UK is in **north-west Europe**.
- About **800 miles (1,300 km)** from the north coast of Scotland to the south coast of England.
- About **320 miles (500 km)** across at the widest part.
- Longest distance on the mainland: **John O'Groats** (north-east Scotland) to **Land's End** (south-west England), about **870 miles (1,400 km)**.

### Major cities

| England | Scotland | Wales | Northern Ireland |
|---|---|---|---|
| **London** (capital) | **Edinburgh** (capital) | **Cardiff** (capital) | **Belfast** (capital) |
| Birmingham, Liverpool, Leeds, Sheffield, Bristol, Manchester, Bradford, Newcastle upon Tyne, Plymouth, Southampton, Norwich | Glasgow, Aberdeen, Dundee, Inverness | Swansea, Newport | Londonderry (Derry) |

### Languages and accents

- **English** has many regional accents and dialects.
  - e.g. **Geordie** (Tyneside), **Scouse** (Liverpool), **Cockney** (London).
- **Welsh** is widely spoken in **Wales** and taught in schools.
- **Gaelic** is spoken in parts of the **Highlands and Islands** of Scotland.
- **Irish Gaelic** is spoken by some people in **Northern Ireland**.
- **Ulster Scots** is spoken in some parts of Northern Ireland.

### Currency

- The **pound sterling** (£). 100 pence (p) = £1.
- Coins: 1p, 2p, 5p, 10p, 20p, 50p, £1, £2.
- Notes: £5, £10, £20, £50.
- **Scotland** and **Northern Ireland** have their **own banknotes**, valid everywhere in the UK.

| Nation | Share of population |
|---|---|
| England | **84%** |

| Wales | **5%** |
| Northern Ireland | **Less than 3%** |

### Trends

- Population growth is due to **longer lives** and **migration**.
- **Ageing population**: record numbers of people aged **85 and over**.
- The **census** has been taken every **10 years since 1801** (except 1941, in WWII). Records are made public after **100 years**.

### Equality

- Women in Britain have **equal rights** to men.
- Women make up about **half the workforce**.
- On average, **girls leave school with better qualifications than boys**.
- Men and women are expected to **share** household and childcare jobs.

## Religion

### Religious identity (2009 Citizenship Survey)

| Religion | % |
|---|---|
| **Christian** | **70%** |
| **Muslim** | 4% |
| **Hindu** | 2% |
| **Sikh** | 1% |
| **Jewish** or **Buddhist** | Less than 0.5% each |
| Other religion | 2% |
| **No religion** | **21%** |

- Everyone has the legal right to **choose their religion** or to have **none**.
- Religious buildings across the UK: churches, cathedrals, mosques, gurdwaras, temples, synagogues.

### The Church of England

- Also called the **Anglican** Church. In the USA and some other countries it is called the **Episcopal** Church.
- It is **Protestant** and has existed since the **Reformation** in the **1530s**.
- It is the **state church** (established church) of **England**.
- The **monarch** is its head (**Supreme Governor**).
- Its spiritual leader is the **Archbishop of Canterbury**.
- The monarch appoints the Archbishop and senior church officials, usually on the **Prime Minister's** advice (who takes advice from a Church committee).
- Several **Church of England bishops** sit in the **House of Lords**.

### The Church of Scotland

- The **national church** of Scotland.
- It is **Presbyterian**: governed by **ministers and elders**.
- Its chairperson is the **Moderator**, appointed for **one year** only.

### Wales and Northern Ireland

- There is **no established (state) church** in **Wales** or **Northern Ireland**.

### Other Christians

- Other Protestant groups: **Baptists, Methodists, Presbyterians, Quakers**.
- **Roman Catholics** are the largest other Christian denomination.

## Customs and traditions

### Christian festivals

| Festival | Date | Meaning / customs |
|---|---|---|
| **Christmas Day** | **25 December** | Birth of **Jesus Christ**. Public holiday. Presents, Christmas tree, roast turkey, Christmas pudding, mince pies. **Father Christmas** (Santa Claus) brings presents. |
| **Boxing Day** | **26 December** | Day after Christmas. Public holiday. |
| **Lent** | 40 days before Easter | Time to fast or give something up. |
| **Shrove Tuesday** ("Pancake Day") | Day **before Lent** starts | People eat **pancakes**. |
| **Ash Wednesday** | **First day of Lent** | |
| **Good Friday** | Friday before Easter | The day **Jesus died** (crucifixion). |
| **Easter Sunday** | March or April | Jesus **rising from the dead**. Easter eggs (chocolate). |

### Festivals of other religions

| Festival | Religion | Facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Diwali** | **Hindus and Sikhs** | October or November. The **Festival of Lights**. Celebrates the victory of **good over evil**. Famous celebration in **Leicester**. |
| **Hannukah** | **Jews** | November or December. Lasts **8 days**. Candles lit on a **menorah**. Remembers the Jews' struggle for religious freedom. |
| **Eid al-Fitr** | **Muslims** | Celebrates the **end of Ramadan** (a month of fasting). |
| **Eid ul Adha** | **Muslims** | Remembers that the **Prophet Ibrahim** was willing to sacrifice his son for God. Many Muslims sacrifice an animal to eat. |
| **Vaisakhi** (Baisakhi) | **Sikhs** | **14 April**. Celebrates the founding of the **Sikh community (the Khalsa)**. Parades and dancing. |

### Other important days

| Day | Date | Facts |
|---|---|---|
| **New Year** | **1 January** | Public holiday. Celebrations on 31 December. |
| **Hogmanay** | **31 December** | **Scotland's** New Year's Eve. **2 January** is also a holiday in Scotland. **"First footing"**: being the first person to visit a home after midnight. |
| **Valentine's Day** | **14 February** | Lovers exchange cards and gifts, sometimes anonymously. |
| **April Fool's Day** | **1 April** | Jokes are played until **midday**. |
| **Mothering Sunday** (Mother's Day) | **3 weeks before Easter** | Cards and gifts for mothers. |
| **Father's Day** | **3rd Sunday in June** | |
| **Halloween** | **31 October** | Ancient festival with roots in a **pagan** festival marking the start of winter. **Pumpkin lanterns**, **"trick or treat"**, costumes. |
| **Bonfire Night** | **5 November** | Remembers the **Gunpowder Plot (1605)**: a Catholic group led by **Guy Fawkes** tried to kill the Protestant **King James I** by **blowing up the Houses of Parliament**. Fireworks and bonfires. |
| **Remembrance Day** | **11 November** | Remembers those who died in wars. People wear **red poppies**. **Two minutes' silence** at **11.00 am**. |

### Bank holidays

- **Early May**, **late May (Spring)** and **end of August (Summer)** bank holidays.
- **Northern Ireland**: also holidays for **St Patrick's Day** and the **Battle of the Boyne** (12 July).
- **Scotland**: **St Andrew's Day** (30 November) and 2 January.

## Sport

### Major sports and events

| Sport | Facts |
|---|---|
| **Cricket** | Started in **England**. Matches can last up to **5 days**. Most famous competition: **the Ashes** (England vs **Australia**). Phrases in English: "rain stopped play", "batting on a sticky wicket", "playing with a straight bat", "bowled a googly". |
| **Football** | The UK's **most popular** sport. England, Scotland, Wales and NI each have **their own team** and league. England's top league is the **Premier League**. England won the **World Cup** only once, in **1966**, hosted in the UK. |
| **Rugby** | Started in **England** in the early 19th century. Two types: **rugby union** and **rugby league**. **Six Nations Championship**: England, Scotland, Wales, Ireland, France, Italy. Rugby league: **Super League**. |
| **Horse racing** | Royal links. **Royal Ascot** (Berkshire), the **Grand National** at **Aintree** (near Liverpool), the **Scottish Grand National** at **Ayr**. **National Horseracing Museum** at **Newmarket**, Suffolk. |
| **Golf** | Started in **Scotland** in the **15th century**. **St Andrews** is the **home of golf**. **The Open Championship** is the main tournament. |
| **Tennis** | Modern tennis developed in **England** in the late 19th century. First tennis club: **Leamington Spa, 1872**. **Wimbledon** (All England Lawn Tennis and Croquet Club) is the **oldest** tennis tournament in the world and the **only Grand Slam played on grass**. |
| **Water sports** | **Sir Francis Chichester**: first to sail **single-handed around the world** (1966–67). **Sir Robin Knox-Johnston**: first to do it **non-stop** (1969). **Cowes**, Isle of Wight: famous sailing event. |
| **Motor sport** | **Formula 1 British Grand Prix**. British world champions: **Damon Hill, Lewis Hamilton, Jenson Button**. |
| **Skiing** | **5 ski centres in Scotland**. Also dry ski slopes and indoor snow centres. |

### The Olympics and Paralympics

- The UK has hosted the Olympics **three times**: **1908, 1948 and 2012**.
- 2012 main site: **Stratford, East London**.
- Team GB came **third** in the 2012 medal table.
- **Paralympics**: began from the work of **Dr Sir Ludwig Guttmann** at **Stoke Mandeville** hospital, **Buckinghamshire**. He treated spinal injuries with sport and organised a competition in **1948**.

## Arts and culture

### Music

#### Classical composers

| Composer | Dates | Known for |
|---|---|---|
| **Henry Purcell** | 1659–1695 | Organist at **Westminster Abbey**. Church music, operas |
| **George Frederick Handel** | 1685–1759 | Born in **Germany**. **Water Music** (for King George I) and **Messiah** |

| **Sir William Walton** | 1902–1983 | Film music; ***Façade***; **coronation marches** for George VI and Elizabeth II |

#### Music events and venues

- **The Proms**: an **8-week** summer season of classical music, held mainly at the **Royal Albert Hall**, London, since **1895**. Ends with the **Last Night of the Proms**.
- Festivals: **Glastonbury**, **Isle of Wight Festival**, **Download** (Donington Park).
- **National Eisteddfod of Wales**: yearly festival of music, dance, art and performance, mostly in **Welsh**.
- Awards: **Mercury Music Prize** (best album), **Brit Awards** (pop).
- Venues: **Wembley Stadium**, the **O2** (London), **SECC** (Glasgow).
- Pop bands: **The Beatles**, **The Rolling Stones**.

### Theatre

- **London's West End** ("Theatreland").
- **The Mousetrap** by **Agatha Christie**: the **longest initial run** of any show in history.
- **Gilbert and Sullivan**: comic operas, e.g. ***HMS Pinafore***, ***The Pirates of Penzance***, ***The Mikado***.
- **Andrew Lloyd Webber**: musicals, e.g. ***Jesus Christ Superstar*** and ***Evita*** (with Tim Rice), ***Cats***, ***The Phantom of the Opera***.
- **Pantomime**: at **Christmas**. Based on fairy stories (e.g. Cinderella, Aladdin). Features the **"Dame"** (a woman played by a man) and a **pantomime horse or cow**.
- **Edinburgh Festival**: every **summer**, includes the **Fringe**, a showcase of mostly comedy and theatre.
- **Laurence Olivier Awards**: theatre awards, named after the actor.

### Cinema

- Films have been made in Britain since the **late 1800s**.
- **Charlie Chaplin**: famous British silent-film actor.
- 1930s: directors like **Sir Alfred Hitchcock** and **Alexander Korda**.
- **Ealing Studios**: claims to be the **oldest continuously working film studio** in the world. Famous for the 1940s–50s **Ealing comedies**, e.g. *Kind Hearts and Coronets*.
- **David Lean**: *Brief Encounter*, *Lawrence of Arabia*.
- Recent successes: *Chariots of Fire*, *Four Weddings and a Funeral*, *Harry Potter*, *James Bond*.
- **Nick Park**: **Wallace and Gromit**. Won **4 Oscars**.
- **BAFTAs**: British film awards.

### Comedy

- From **medieval court jesters** to modern stand-up.
- **Punch and Judy** puppet shows.
- 19th century: **music halls** and the satirical magazine ***Punch***.
- **Monty Python's Flying Circus** (1960s–70s TV).

### TV and radio

- The **BBC** is funded by the **TV licence**, not by adverts.
- You need a **TV licence** to watch live TV on any device.
- Fine for watching without a licence: up to **£1,000**.
- Other channels: **ITV, Channel 4, Channel 5, Sky** (funded by adverts or subscriptions).

### Pubs, alcohol and gambling

- You must be **18** to **buy alcohol** or drink it in a pub.
- **16–17-year-olds** can drink **wine or beer with a meal** in a hotel or restaurant if **with someone over 18**.
- You must be **18** to go into **betting shops** or gambling clubs.
- ⚠️ Handbook: **16** for the **National Lottery**. Now **18** (since 2021).

### Pets

- **Dogs** in public must wear a **collar** with the **owner's name and address**.
- Owners must keep dogs **under control** and **clean up** after them.
- It is **illegal** to treat a pet **cruelly** or **neglect** it.
- Vets are listed in local directories. The **PDSA** helps owners who can't afford a vet.

### Days out

- There are **15 national parks** in England, Wales and Scotland.
- **The National Trust**: a charity founded in **1895** by **three volunteers**. It looks after historic buildings and the countryside.
- The **National Trust for Scotland** does the same in Scotland.
