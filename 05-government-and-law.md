## The British constitution

- **Not written down in one document** ("unwritten" or uncodified).
- Made up of **laws, conventions (customs) and court rulings**.
- The UK is a **parliamentary democracy** with a **constitutional monarch** as head of state.

### Key institutions

- The monarchy
- Parliament (**House of Commons** and **House of Lords**)
- The Prime Minister and Cabinet
- The **judiciary** (judges)
- The police
- The **civil service**
- Local government
- Devolved governments (Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland)

---

## The monarchy

- The UK is a **constitutional monarchy**: the monarch **does not rule** the country.
- The monarch is **head of state** of the UK and of many **Commonwealth** countries.
- The monarch is head of the **Church of England**.

### What the monarch does

- **Appoints the Prime Minister** (the leader of the party with most MPs).
- Opens each new session of Parliament with the **King's Speech** (⚠️ "Queen's Speech" in the handbook). It sets out the government's plans.
- Signs laws (**Royal Assent**) after they pass Parliament.
- Meets the Prime Minister regularly. Has the right to **advise, warn and be consulted**.
- Represents the UK on visits abroad and ceremonies at home.
- Must stay **politically neutral**.

### The royal family

- ⚠️ **Handbook**: **Queen Elizabeth II**, reigned from **1952**. Celebrated her **Diamond Jubilee** (60 years) in **2012**. Married to **Prince Philip**. Heir: **Prince Charles**, the **Prince of Wales**.
- **Now**: **King Charles III** since **8 September 2022**. Heir: **Prince William**, the **Prince of Wales**.

### The national anthem

> God save our gracious King!
> Long live our noble King!
> God save the King!
> Send him victorious,
> Happy and glorious,
> Long to reign over us,
> God save the King!

⚠️ The handbook version uses "Queen" and "her".

## Parliament

### The House of Commons

- The **more important** of the two Houses.
- Members are **MPs** (Members of Parliament), **elected** by the public.
- The UK is divided into **650 constituencies**. Each elects **one MP**.
- MPs:
  - represent everyone in their constituency
  - help make new **laws**
  - **scrutinise** and comment on what the government does
  - **debate** important issues

### The Speaker

- Chairs debates in the House of Commons.
- **Politically neutral**.
- **Chosen by other MPs** in a **secret ballot**.
- Keeps order and makes sure rules are followed.
- Represents Parliament on ceremonial occasions.
- Is still an MP for a constituency.

### Whips

- A small group of MPs appointed by party leaders.
- Responsible for **discipline** in the party and making sure MPs **attend and vote**.

### The House of Lords

- Members are called **peers**. They are **not elected**.
- Historically: **hereditary** peers, senior **judges** and **Church of England bishops**.
- **1958**: the PM could appoint **life peers** (they cannot pass the title on).
- Life peers are appointed by the **monarch on the advice of the Prime Minister**. They include people from other parties, chosen by their leaders.
- **1999**: hereditary peers lost the automatic right to sit. A small number now elect some of their own to remain.
- The Lords is **independent** of the Commons.
- It can **suggest amendments** or **propose new laws**, which MPs then discuss.
- It **checks** laws passed by the Commons.
- The **Commons can overrule the Lords** if needed.

### Visiting Parliament

- The public can watch debates from the **public galleries** of both Houses (queue on the day, or arrange through your MP).
- **Hansard** is the official written record of what is said in Parliament.
- You can visit the **Scottish Parliament**, **Senedd** and **NI Assembly** too.

## Elections

### General elections

- Held **at least every 5 years**.
- All **650 MPs** are elected.
- Voting system: **"first past the post"**: the candidate with the **most votes** in a constituency wins.
- The party with the **most MPs** usually forms the government.
- If no party has a majority, parties may form a **coalition**.

### By-elections

- Held when an MP **dies or resigns** between general elections.

### Contacting your MP

- Write to them at their constituency office or at the **House of Commons, Westminster, London SW1A 0AA**.
- Phone them.
- Visit a **"surgery"**: a local meeting where you can see your MP in person.
- Find contact details at **parliament.uk**.

## The government

### The Prime Minister (PM)

- The **leader** of the party in power.
- **Appoints Cabinet ministers**.
- Controls many important **public appointments**.
- Official home: **10 Downing Street**, London.
- Country house: **Chequers**, Buckinghamshire.
- Can be **changed** if the MPs in their party decide (without a general election).

### The Cabinet

- About **20 senior MPs** (and sometimes peers) chosen by the PM.
- Meets usually **weekly**.
- Makes important decisions on government policy.
- Decisions often need **approval by Parliament**.

| Minister | Responsible for |
|---|---|
| **Chancellor of the Exchequer** | The **economy** (money, tax, spending) |
| **Home Secretary** | **Crime, policing and immigration** |
| **Foreign Secretary** | **Relations with foreign countries** |
| Other Secretaries of State | Education, health, defence, etc. |

### The opposition

- The **second-largest party** in the House of Commons.
- Its leader is the **Leader of the Opposition**.
- Appoints senior MPs to a **"Shadow Cabinet"**.
- Points out the government's failures and weaknesses.
- Important moment: **Prime Minister's Questions (PMQs)**, held **every week** while Parliament is sitting.

### The civil service

- Civil servants **carry out government policy**.
- They are **politically neutral** (not political appointments).
- Chosen **on merit**.
- They must serve whichever party is in power.

## Local government

- Towns, cities and rural areas are run by **elected councils** (local authorities).
- Some areas have **two tiers**: county councils and district councils.
- **London** has **33** local authorities. The **Greater London Authority** and the **Mayor of London** coordinate them.
- Councils provide: **education**, **housing**, **social services**, **refuse collection** (bins), **libraries**, **roads**, planning, environmental health.
- Money comes from **central government** and from **Council Tax**.
- Many councils have a **ceremonial mayor**. Some places have an **elected mayor** who actually leads the council.
- **Local elections** are normally held in **May** every year.

## Devolved governments

Since **1997**, some powers have been **devolved** (passed) from Westminster to Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

- Policy on **defence, foreign affairs, immigration, taxation** and **social security** stays with the **UK Parliament** (these are "reserved" matters).

### Comparison table

| | Scotland | Wales | Northern Ireland |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Name** | **Scottish Parliament** | ⚠️ Handbook: **National Assembly for Wales**. Now **Senedd Cymru / Welsh Parliament** | **Northern Ireland Assembly** |
| **Location** | **Edinburgh** (Holyrood) | **Cardiff** (the Senedd building) | **Belfast** (Stormont) |
| **Created** | **1999** | **1999** | **1999** (after the **1998** Good Friday Agreement) |
| **Members** | **129 MSPs** (Members of the Scottish Parliament) | ⚠️ Handbook: **60 AMs**. Now MSs; 96 from 2026 | ⚠️ Handbook: **108 MLAs**. Now **90** |
| **Voting system** | Proportional representation | Proportional representation | Proportional representation |
| **Leader** | First Minister | First Minister | **First Minister and deputy First Minister** (power-sharing) |
| **Powers** | Can pass laws on everything **not reserved** to Westminster, e.g. civil and criminal law, health, education, planning; some tax powers | Education and training, health and social services, economic development, housing, and more | Education, agriculture, environment, health, social services |

Key facts:

- In **Wales**, both **Welsh and English** are used in the Senedd.
- The **NI Assembly** shares power between the main **unionist and nationalist** parties.
- The **UK government can suspend** the NI Assembly if politicians can't work together. This has happened several times.

## The media and politics

- The UK has a **free press**: the government does not control newspapers.
- Newspaper owners and editors can hold **strong political views**.
- **Broadcasters** (TV and radio) must give **balanced coverage** of all parties by law.
- **Party political broadcasts** are given free to parties before elections.

## Voting

### Who can vote

| Election | Who can vote |
|---|---|
| **All public elections** (incl. general elections) | **UK citizens**, plus citizens of the **Commonwealth** and the **Irish Republic** living in the UK |
| **Local and devolved elections** | The above, plus (⚠️ handbook) citizens of other **EU** countries living in the UK. Rules have changed since Brexit. |

- **Voting age: 18**.
- ⚠️ **16-year-olds** can now vote in **Scottish Parliament**, **Senedd** and **local elections in Scotland and Wales**.
- You must be on the **electoral register** to vote.

### The electoral register

- Register online or by form from your **local council**.
- The register is updated **every year** (an annual form is sent in the autumn).
- In **Northern Ireland**, everyone must register **individually**.
- **Anyone** can look at the electoral register, under **supervision**, at the local electoral registration office (or some libraries).

### How to vote

- At a **polling station** (or **polling place** in Scotland).
- Open **7.00 am to 10.00 pm** on election day.
- You get a **poll card** telling you where to go. You don't need it to vote.
- Voting is by **secret ballot**.
- You can also vote by **post** (postal vote) or by **proxy** (someone votes for you).
- ⚠️ Since 2023, you need **photo ID** to vote at polling stations in Great Britain (NI already required it).

### Standing for office

- Most citizens of the **UK, Irish Republic or Commonwealth** aged **18 or over** can stand.
- **Cannot stand**: members of the **armed forces**, **civil servants**, people found guilty of certain **criminal offences**.
- Members of the **House of Lords** cannot stand for the House of Commons.

## The UK and international organisations

| Organisation | Facts |
|---|---|
| **The Commonwealth** | Association of countries, mostly **former British territories**. ⚠️ Handbook: **54** members (now **56**). The **monarch is head**. Supports **democracy, good government and the rule of law**. Membership is **voluntary**; it has **no power** over members. |
| **The United Nations (UN)** | Set up after **WWII** to prevent war and promote peace. The UK is one of **five permanent members** of the **Security Council** (with the **USA, France, Russia and China**). |
| **NATO** | North Atlantic Treaty Organization. European and North American countries that agree to **help each other if attacked**. The UK is a member. |
| **The Council of Europe** | **Separate from the EU**. ⚠️ Handbook: **47** members (now **46**). The UK is a member. **Cannot make laws** but draws up **conventions**, e.g. the **European Convention on Human Rights**. |
| **The European Union (EU)** | Founded by **6 countries** signing the **Treaty of Rome (25 March 1957)**: Belgium, France, West Germany, Italy, Luxembourg, the Netherlands. The UK **joined in 1973**. ⚠️ The UK **left on 31 January 2020**. |

## Respecting the law

### Criminal law vs civil law

| Criminal law | Civil law |
|---|---|
| Crimes, investigated by the **police**, punished by the courts | Settles **disputes** between people or groups |
| Examples: carrying a **weapon**, **drug** dealing, **racially motivated** crime, **selling tobacco** or **alcohol** to under-18s, **smoking in public places** | Examples: **housing** law (landlords and tenants), **consumer rights** (faulty goods), **employment** law (unfair dismissal), **debt** |

### More crimes to know

- Carrying a **weapon** (even for self-defence) is a crime.
- Selling or buying **tobacco** products under **18**.
- Buying **alcohol** for someone under 18.
- **Smoking** in public enclosed places (pubs, restaurants, etc.).
- **Racial** crime, e.g. abuse or attacks based on race.
- Behaving in an **anti-social** way in public, e.g. drunk and disorderly.
- **Hate crime** based on religion, sexual orientation or disability.

### The police

The police must:

- **Protect life and property**
- **Prevent disturbances** (keep the peace)
- **Prevent and detect crime**

Facts:

- Each area has its own police force. The **Metropolitan Police** covers London.
- Since **November 2012**, people in England and Wales elect **Police and Crime Commissioners (PCCs)**.
- **PCSOs** (Police Community Support Officers) support police officers.
- The police are **independent** of the government and must **obey the law**.

### Complaints against the police

| Area | Who handles complaints |
|---|---|
| England and Wales | ⚠️ Handbook: **Independent Police Complaints Commission (IPCC)**. Now the **IOPC** |
| Scotland | **Police Investigations and Review Commissioner** |
| Northern Ireland | **Police Ombudsman** |

You can also complain to the **chief constable** of the police force.

### Terrorism and extremism

- Report suspicious activity to the police or the **anti-terrorist hotline**.

## The courts

### The judiciary

- **Judges** are **independent** of government.
- They interpret the law and make sure trials are fair.
- If a judge finds that a public body is not respecting legal rights, they can order it to change.
- Judges cannot overturn Acts of Parliament.

### Criminal courts

| Court | England & Wales | Northern Ireland | Scotland |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Minor crimes** | **Magistrates' Court** | **Magistrates' Court** | **Justice of the Peace Court** |
| **Who decides** | Usually **3 magistrates** (JPs): unpaid, no legal qualifications | A **District Judge or Deputy District Judge**: legally qualified and paid | **Justices of the Peace** (JPs): unpaid, no legal qualifications |
| **Serious crimes** | **Crown Court** | **Crown Court** | **Sheriff Court** |
| **Most serious** (e.g. murder) | Crown Court | Crown Court | **High Court** |
| **Jury size** | **12** | **12** | **15** |
| **Young people** | **Youth Court** (ages **10–17**) | **Youth Court** (ages **10–17**) | **Children's Hearings System** |

Key facts:

- A **jury** is made up of members of the **public** chosen at random.
- The jury decides **"guilty" or "not guilty"**. In Scotland there is a third verdict, **"not proven"** (being abolished).
- The **judge** decides the **sentence**.
- In Youth Courts, the public is not allowed in and the accused's name cannot be published.

### Civil courts

| Court | Where |
|---|---|
| **County Court** | England, Wales and Northern Ireland: most civil disputes (e.g. debt, contracts, divorce) |
| **Sheriff Court** | Scotland: most civil cases |
| **High Court** / **Court of Session** | More serious civil cases (Court of Session is in **Scotland**) |

### Small claims procedure

- An informal way to settle **small disputes** (e.g. faulty goods) without a lawyer.
- ⚠️ Handbook limits: under **£5,000** in **England and Wales**, under **£3,000** in **Scotland and NI**.
- Current limit in England and Wales: **£10,000**.
- Held in a room with a judge, not a courtroom.

### Legal advice

- **Solicitors** give legal advice and can represent you in court.
- **Citizens Advice** gives free advice.
- **Legal aid** may help pay legal costs if you have a low income.

### Jury service

- Anyone on the **electoral register** aged **18 to 70** can be called (⚠️ now up to **75** in England and Wales).
- You **must** do it unless you have a good reason.

## Fundamental rights

### The European Convention on Human Rights

- Drawn up by the **Council of Europe**. British lawyers helped write it.
- Brought into UK law by the **Human Rights Act 1998**.

Rights include:

- **Right to life**
- Prohibition of **torture**
- Prohibition of **slavery** and forced labour
- Right to **liberty** and security
- Right to a **fair trial**
- Freedom of **thought, conscience and religion**
- Freedom of **expression** (speech)
- Prohibition of **discrimination**
- Right to **marry** and found a family

### Equal opportunities

- **Equality Act 2010** (England, Wales and Scotland). NI has similar laws.
- It is illegal to discriminate because of:
  - age
  - disability
  - sex
  - pregnancy and maternity
  - race
  - religion or belief
  - sexuality (sexual orientation)
  - marital status (incl. civil partnership)
  - gender reassignment

| Area | Equality body |
|---|---|
| England, Wales, Scotland | **Equality and Human Rights Commission** |
| Scotland (human rights) | **Scottish Human Rights Commission** |
| Northern Ireland | **Equality Commission for Northern Ireland** and **Northern Ireland Human Rights Commission** |

### Protecting people

- **Domestic violence** is a serious crime, between men and women alike.
  - A man who **forces** a woman to have sex, **including his wife**, can be charged with **rape**.
  - Help: refuges, the **National Domestic Violence Helpline**, police.
- **Female genital mutilation (FGM)** is **illegal**. It is also a crime to **arrange** FGM **in or outside** the UK.
- **Forced marriage** (one or both people do not consent) is **illegal**.
  - **Arranged marriages** (both agree) are legal.
  - **Forced Marriage Protection Orders**: **2008** in England, Wales and NI; **2011** in Scotland.
  - Breaking an order can lead to **prison** (up to 2 years).

## Taxation

### Income tax

- Paid on: **wages**, **self-employed** profits, **taxable benefits**, **pensions**, **rental income**, **savings** and **dividends**.
- Most employees pay through **PAYE** (**Pay As You Earn**): tax is taken from wages by the employer.
- **Self-employed** people fill in a **self-assessment** tax return.
- Collected by **HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC)**.

### National Insurance

- Paid by almost everyone in work, including the self-employed.
- Pays for **state benefits** and the **NHS**.
- Contributions qualify you for the **State Pension**.
- If you don't pay enough, you may not get some benefits.
- **National Insurance number**: a unique personal number. Young people get theirs just before they turn **16**. You need one to work.

### Other taxes

- **VAT** (Value Added Tax), excise duties, **Council Tax** (to local councils).

## Driving

- Minimum age: **17** to drive a **car or motorcycle**. (**16** for a moped.)
- You need a **driving licence**.
- Start with a **provisional** licence. Pass a **theory test** and a **practical test**.
- Licence is valid **until you are 70**. Then renew it **every 3 years**.
- **Northern Ireland**: new drivers display an **"R" plate** for **1 year** after passing (the handbook says "R" is for "registered driver"; officially it means "restricted").
- Drivers with a licence from another country can use it for **12 months** (EU licences: until they expire).
- **Car insurance** is **compulsory**. Driving without it is a **criminal offence**.
- Cars over **3 years old** need an **MOT** test **every year**.
- You must pay **vehicle tax**.
- Police can stop you to test for **alcohol** (breath test) or check your documents.

## Your role in the community

### Values and responsibilities

- Obey the law.
- Respect others.
- Look after yourself and your family.
- Look after your local area and the environment.

### Being a good neighbour

- Introduce yourself to neighbours.
- Keep noise down and respect privacy.
- **Neighbourhood Watch** schemes help prevent crime.

### Ways to get involved

| Way | Facts |
|---|---|
| **Jury service** | See above |
| **School governor** (England, Wales, NI) / **school board** member (Scotland) | Help set the school's strategy and hold the head teacher to account |
| **Parent-Teacher Association (PTA)** | Supports the school, organises events and fundraising |
| **Political party** | Hand out leaflets, help at elections |
| **Magistrate / JP** | Volunteer to judge minor cases |
| **Special constable** | Volunteer police officer |
| **Volunteering** | e.g. charity shops, helping the homeless, working with children, environmental projects |
| **Blood and organ donation** | Register with the blood service and organ donor register |

- **National Citizen Service (NCS)**: a programme for **16–17-year-olds** to do community activities and outdoor activities.
- Charities and voluntary groups: e.g. **NSPCC** (children), **RSPCA** (animals), **Shelter** (homelessness), **Cancer Research UK**.
- **Free schools**: government-funded schools run by parents, teachers, charities or businesses.

### Protecting the environment

- **Recycle** as much as possible.
- **Buy local** products.
- **Walk**, **cycle** or use **public transport**. Share cars.
- Don't **litter**. It is a crime to drop litter.
