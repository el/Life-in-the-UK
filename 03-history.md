## Early Britain

### The Stone Age

- First people were **hunter-gatherers**.
- Britain was joined to Europe by a **land bridge**.
- Britain became an island about **10,000 years ago**, when the Channel formed.
- The first **farmers** arrived about **6,000 years ago**, probably from **south-east Europe**.

### Famous prehistoric sites

| Site | Location | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Stonehenge** | Wiltshire, England | Stone Age monument, probably a special gathering place for seasonal ceremonies |
| **Skara Brae** | Orkney, Scotland | Best-preserved prehistoric village in **northern Europe** |

### The Bronze Age (about 4,000 years ago)

- People learnt to make **bronze**.
- Lived in **roundhouses**.
- Buried their dead in **round barrows**.
- Skilled metalworkers: tools, ornaments and weapons.

### The Iron Age

- People learnt to make **iron**.
- Lived in roundhouses grouped into **hill forts**.
- Example: **Maiden Castle**, Dorset.
- Spoke a **Celtic** language.
- The **first coins** were made in Britain, some with names of **Iron Age kings** on them.

⭐ **Must-know:** Stonehenge (Wiltshire) · Skara Brae (Orkney) · Maiden Castle (Dorset) · first coins in the Iron Age

## The Romans

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **55 BC** | **Julius Caesar** led an invasion. It **failed**. |
| **AD 43** | **Emperor Claudius** led a **successful** invasion. |
| **AD 410** | Roman army left to defend other parts of the Empire. |

- The Romans stayed for about **400 years**.
- **Boudicca**, queen of the **Iceni** in eastern England, fought against the Romans.
  - Her **statue** is on **Westminster Bridge**, London, near the Houses of Parliament.
- The Romans did **not conquer Scotland**.
- **Emperor Hadrian** built **Hadrian's Wall** in the north of England to keep out the **Picts** (ancestors of the Scottish people).
  - Famous parts: forts at **Housesteads** and **Vindolanda**.
  - It is a **UNESCO World Heritage Site**.

### What the Romans brought

- **Roads** and public **buildings**
- A **structure of law**
- New **plants and animals**
- The first **Christian communities** (3rd and 4th centuries AD)

⭐ **Must-know:** 55 BC Caesar failed · AD 43 Claudius succeeded · Boudicca (Iceni) · Hadrian's Wall vs Picts · left AD 410

## Anglo-Saxons and Vikings

### Anglo-Saxons

- Tribes from northern Europe: the **Jutes, Angles and Saxons**.
- By about **AD 600**, Anglo-Saxon kingdoms covered most of Britain.
- They did **not** conquer **Wales** and **Scotland** (these areas stayed free of their rule).
- **Sutton Hoo** (Suffolk): burial site of a king, buried with treasure and armour in a **ship**.
- Their language is the basis of modern **English**.

### Christianity returns

| Missionary | Fact |
|---|---|
| **St Patrick** | Patron saint of **Ireland** |
| **St Columba** | Founded a monastery on **Iona**, off the coast of Scotland |
| **St Augustine** | Came from **Rome**. Became the **first Archbishop of Canterbury** |

### Vikings

- Came from **Denmark and Norway**.
- First visited in **AD 789**, raiding coastal towns and taking people as slaves.
- **King Alfred the Great** united the Anglo-Saxon kingdoms and **defeated the Vikings**.
- Many Vikings settled in the east and north of England, an area called the **Danelaw**.
- Viking place names: **Grimsby**, **Scunthorpe**.
- **Cnut** (Canute), a Danish king, later ruled England.
- In the north, the Viking threat pushed people to unite under one king, **Kenneth MacAlpin**. This became **Scotland**.

⭐ **Must-know:** Jutes, Angles, Saxons · Sutton Hoo · St Augustine = first Archbishop of Canterbury · Vikings from Denmark & Norway · Alfred the Great · Danelaw · Kenneth MacAlpin

## The Norman Conquest (1066)

- **1066**: **William, Duke of Normandy** (France) defeated **King Harold** (Saxon) at the **Battle of Hastings**.
- Harold was killed in the battle.
- William became king: **William the Conqueror**.
- The battle is shown on the **Bayeux Tapestry** (still in **France**).
- This was the **last successful foreign invasion** of England.

### Effects

- Norman **French** became the language of the king's court. It mixed with Anglo-Saxon to form modern English.
- **Feudalism**: the king gave land to lords in return for help in war; peasants worked the land.
- The Normans also conquered **Wales**, but **not Scotland**.
- The **Domesday Book**: William's record of towns and villages, who lived there, the land and the animals. It still exists.

⭐ **Must-know:** 1066 · Battle of Hastings · Bayeux Tapestry · last successful invasion · Domesday Book

## The Middle Ages

The Middle Ages (Medieval period) lasted from **1066 to 1485**.

### War with Wales, Scotland and Ireland

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1284** | **Edward I** brought in the **Statute of Rhuddlan**, joining Wales to the Crown of England. Castles like **Conwy** and **Caernarvon** were built. |
| **1314** | **Battle of Bannockburn**: the Scots, led by **Robert the Bruce**, defeated the English. Scotland stayed unconquered. |

- Last Welsh rebellion was defeated by the **middle of the 15th century**.
- In Ireland, the English controlled only an area around **Dublin** called **the Pale**.

### War abroad

- **Crusades**: European Christians fought for control of the **Holy Land**.
- **Hundred Years War** with France: actually lasted **116 years** (1337 to 1453).
- **Battle of Agincourt (1415)**: **King Henry V**'s outnumbered English army won.
- The English left France by the **1450s**.

### The Black Death (1348)

- A plague that killed about **one third** of the population of England (similar in Scotland and Wales).
- Afterwards there were **fewer workers**, so:
  - Peasants demanded **higher wages**.
  - A new class, the **gentry**, owned land.
  - People moved to **towns**.
  - Less land was farmed for crops; more was used for animals.

### Magna Carta (1215)

- Also called **the Great Charter**.
- **King John** was forced by his **noblemen** to agree to it.
- It established the idea that **even the king was subject to the law**.
- It protected the rights of the nobles and limited the king's power to **collect taxes** or **change laws**.

### The start of Parliament

| England | Scotland |
|---|---|
| **House of Lords**: nobility, great landowners, bishops | Parliament had three **Estates**: |
| **House of Commons**: knights and wealthy people from towns and cities | the **lords**, the **commons** and the **clergy** |

### Law

- Judges began to be **independent** of the government.
- In England, judges developed **"common law"**: decisions based on **precedent** (past decisions) and tradition.
- Scotland developed its own, different legal system.

### Language and literature

- English became the language of the official documents and Parliament (it was a mix of Norman French and Anglo-Saxon).
- **Geoffrey Chaucer** wrote **The Canterbury Tales**: stories told by **pilgrims travelling to Canterbury**.
- **William Caxton** was the **first person in England to print books** using a printing press. *The Canterbury Tales* was one of the first books he printed.
- In Scotland, many people spoke **Scots**. **John Barbour** wrote **The Bruce**, a poem about Bannockburn.

### Buildings and trade

- Many **castles** were built (e.g. in Wales after Edward I).
- Great **cathedrals**, e.g. **Lincoln Cathedral** and **York Minster** (famous stained glass).
- England was known for its **wool** and cloth. Skilled workers came from abroad, e.g. weavers from France, engineers from Germany, glass makers from Italy, canal builders from Holland.

### The Wars of the Roses (1455–1485)

| House | Symbol |
|---|---|
| **Lancaster** | **Red** rose |
| **York** | **White** rose |

- Ended at the **Battle of Bosworth Field (1485)**.
- **Henry Tudor** (Lancaster) killed **King Richard III** (York).
- Henry became **King Henry VII** and married **Elizabeth of York**, uniting the two houses.
- The **Tudor rose** (red and white) symbolises this.

⭐ **Must-know:** 1215 Magna Carta · 1284 Statute of Rhuddlan · 1314 Bannockburn · 1348 Black Death · 1415 Agincourt · 1455 Wars of the Roses · 1485 Bosworth · Chaucer & Caxton

## The Tudors

### Henry VII

- Strengthened **central government**.
- Reduced the power of the **nobles**.
- Built up the monarchy's **finances**.

### Henry VIII: famous for six wives and breaking from Rome

| # | Wife | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Catherine of Aragon** | Spanish princess. Had daughter **Mary**. **Divorced**. |
| 2 | **Anne Boleyn** | English. Had daughter **Elizabeth**. **Executed** at the Tower of London. |
| 3 | **Jane Seymour** | Gave Henry his only son, **Edward**. **Died** soon after the birth. |
| 4 | **Anne of Cleves** | German princess. Married briefly, then **divorced**. |
| 5 | **Catherine Howard** | Cousin of Anne Boleyn. **Executed**. |
| 6 | **Catherine Parr** | A widow. **Outlived** Henry. |

Memory aid: **"Divorced, beheaded, died, divorced, beheaded, survived."**

- The **Pope refused** to let Henry divorce Catherine of Aragon.
- So Henry created the **Church of England**, with the **king as head**, not the Pope.
- This was part of the **Reformation**: the **Protestant** movement against the Pope's authority.
- Wales was formally joined to England by the **Act for the Government of Wales**. Welsh representatives sat in the House of Commons.
- Henry declared himself **King of Ireland**.

### Edward VI

- Strongly **Protestant**.
- The **Book of Common Prayer** was written during his reign (still used in the Church of England today).
- **Died aged 15**.

### Mary I ("Bloody Mary")

- A devout **Catholic**.
- **Persecuted Protestants**.
- Died after a short reign.

### Elizabeth I

- **Protestant**. Re-established the **Church of England**.
- **Never married**, had **no children**.
- Very popular.
- **1588**: the English defeated the **Spanish Armada**, a large fleet of Spanish ships sent to invade.
- **Sir Francis Drake**: a commander against the Armada; his ship the **Golden Hind** was one of the first to sail around the world.
- English settlers began to colonise the **east coast of America**.

### Scotland: Mary, Queen of Scots

- Scotland also became **Protestant** (the Scottish Parliament abolished the Pope's authority).
- **Mary Stuart** (Mary, Queen of Scots) was **Catholic**. Her son was **James VI** of Scotland.
- She fled to England. **Elizabeth I** kept her prisoner for **20 years**, then **executed** her for plotting against her.

### William Shakespeare (1564–1616)

- Born in **Stratford-upon-Avon**.
- Playwright and actor. Widely seen as the **greatest writer in the English language**.
- His plays were performed at the **Globe Theatre**, London (now rebuilt).
- Famous plays: *A Midsummer Night's Dream*, *Hamlet*, *Macbeth*, *Romeo and Juliet*.

| Famous line | Play |
|---|---|
| "To be or not to be" | Hamlet |
| "A rose by any other name would smell as sweet" | Romeo and Juliet |
| "Once more unto the breach" | Henry V |
| "All the world's a stage" | As You Like It |
| "We are such stuff as dreams are made on" | The Tempest |

### Ireland

- Protestant settlers were sent to **Ulster** (the north of Ireland) in **"plantations"**.
- This later caused long-lasting conflict.

⭐ **Must-know:** Henry VIII's six wives · Church of England · Reformation · 1588 Spanish Armada · Shakespeare (Stratford-upon-Avon)

## The Stuarts and the Civil War

### James I (James VI of Scotland)

- **1603**: Elizabeth I died with no children. **James VI of Scotland** became **James I of England**.
- He ruled England, Wales, Scotland and Ireland, but they stayed **separate countries**.
- The **King James Bible** (Authorised Version) was produced in his reign.
- **1605**: the **Gunpowder Plot** (see Bonfire Night in [chapter 4](04-modern-society.md)).

### Charles I and the Civil War

- Believed in the **"Divine Right of Kings"**: that kings are chosen by God.
- Tried to rule **without Parliament**.
- Tried to force a new prayer book on Scotland. The **Scots rebelled**.
- He needed money for an army, so he had to recall Parliament.

| Side | Nickname |
|---|---|
| The king's supporters | **Cavaliers** |
| Parliament's supporters | **Roundheads** |

- **1642**: the **Civil War** began.
- Parliament won the battles of **Marston Moor (1644)** and **Naseby (1645)**.
- **1649**: **Charles I was executed**.

### The Commonwealth (1649–1660)

- England became a **republic** called **the Commonwealth**.
- **Oliver Cromwell** was in charge, with the title **Lord Protector**.
- He went to **Ireland** and was very harsh.
- **Charles II** (son of Charles I) was accepted by the Scots, but was defeated at **Worcester**. He escaped by **hiding in an oak tree** (the "Royal Oak").
- Cromwell died in **1658**. His son could not hold power.

### The Restoration (1660)

- **1660**: Parliament invited **Charles II** back from exile in the **Netherlands**.
- **1665**: the **Great Plague** in London.
- **1666**: the **Great Fire of London** destroyed much of the city, including many churches.
- **Sir Christopher Wren** rebuilt **St Paul's Cathedral** and many churches.
- **Samuel Pepys** wrote a famous **diary** describing these events.
- **1679**: the **Habeas Corpus Act**: no one can be held prisoner unlawfully. Every prisoner has the **right to a court hearing**.
- The **Royal Society** was formed: the **oldest surviving scientific society in the world**.

### Sir Isaac Newton (1643–1727)

- Born in **Lincolnshire**.
- Most famous work: ***Principia Mathematica*** (1687), explaining **gravity**.
- Showed that **white light** is made up of the **colours of the rainbow**.

### The Glorious Revolution (1688)

- **James II** (Charles II's brother) was **Catholic**. People feared a return to Catholicism.
- Protestant **William of Orange** (from the Netherlands), married to James's daughter **Mary**, invaded.
- James fled to **France**. There was no fighting in England, so it was called the **"Glorious Revolution"**.
- **William III and Mary II** ruled jointly.
- **1690**: William defeated James at the **Battle of the Boyne** in Ireland.
- **1692**: the **Glencoe Massacre** in Scotland.

### The Bill of Rights (1689)

- Confirmed the **rights of Parliament** and the **limits of the king's power**.
- Parliament took control of **who could be monarch**. The monarch had to be **Protestant**.
- Start of **constitutional monarchy**: the monarch reigns but Parliament governs.
- Two main groups in Parliament: the **Whigs** and the **Tories**. (The Conservative Party is still called the Tories.)

### Other changes

- **1695**: newspapers could be published **without a government licence** (freedom of the press).
- **Huguenots**: French **Protestants** who fled religious persecution (1680–1720). Many were skilled in **science, banking and weaving**.

⭐ **Must-know:** 1603 James I · 1605 Gunpowder Plot · 1642 Civil War · 1649 Charles I executed · Cromwell · 1660 Restoration · 1666 Great Fire · 1679 Habeas Corpus · 1688 Glorious Revolution · 1689 Bill of Rights

## The 18th century (1700s)

### The Act of Union (1707)

- Created the **Kingdom of Great Britain**: England, Wales and Scotland.
- Scotland kept its own:
  - **Legal system**
  - **Education system**
  - **Presbyterian Church** (Church of Scotland)

### The first Prime Minister

- **Queen Anne** had no surviving children.
- **1714**: **George I**, a German, became king.
- He did not speak good English, so ministers became more important.
- **Sir Robert Walpole** was the **first Prime Minister**, from **1721 to 1742**.

### The Jacobite rebellions

- Jacobites supported the Catholic Stuarts (descendants of James II).
- **1745**: **Charles Edward Stuart** (**"Bonnie Prince Charlie"**), grandson of James II, landed in Scotland.
- **1746**: he was defeated at the **Battle of Culloden**. He escaped back to Europe.
- The Scottish **clans** lost power.

### The Highland Clearances

- Scottish landlords destroyed small farms (**crofts**) to make room for **sheep and cattle**.
- Many Scots left for **North America**.

### Robert Burns (1759–1796)

- Scottish poet, known as **"The Bard"**.
- Wrote in **Scots**, English and a mix of both.
- Wrote **"Auld Lang Syne"**, sung at **New Year (Hogmanay)** around the world.

### The Enlightenment

New ideas in politics, philosophy and science. Many leading thinkers were **Scottish**:

| Thinker | Field |
|---|---|
| **Adam Smith** | Economics |
| **David Hume** | Human nature (philosophy) |
| **James Watt** | Steam power (science) |

- Key idea: everyone should have the right to their own **political and religious beliefs**.

⭐ **Must-know:** 1707 Act of Union · Walpole first PM (1721) · 1745/46 Bonnie Prince Charlie & Culloden · Highland Clearances · Robert Burns · Enlightenment thinkers

## The Industrial Revolution and Empire

### The Industrial Revolution (1700s–1800s)

- Britain was the **first country to industrialise**.
- Machines replaced work by hand. Factories grew. People moved from the countryside to cities.
- **Coal** and **iron** were essential.
- **Canals** were built to move goods. Later, **railways**.
- Working conditions were very poor. **Children** worked long hours.

| Person | Achievement |
|---|---|
| **Richard Arkwright** | Improved the **carding** machine (first horse-driven, then **water-powered**). Developed the **factory** system and efficient management. |
| **James Watt** | Developed **steam** power |
| **Henry Bessemer** | The **Bessemer process**: mass production of **steel** |
| **George and Robert Stephenson** | Pioneers of **railway** engines |

### Sake Dean Mahomet (1759–1851)

- Born in **Bengal**, India. Came to Britain in 1782.
- Opened the **first curry house** in Britain: the **Hindoostane Coffee House**, George Street, London (**1810**).
- With his wife, introduced **"shampooing"** (Indian head massage) to Britain.

### Empire and trade

- The **East India Company** controlled large parts of **India**.
- Britain gained colonies in **Canada**, the **Caribbean**, **Australia** and more.

### The slave trade

- Britain made money from the **transatlantic slave trade**. Ships took slaves from **West Africa** to America and the Caribbean.
- **Quakers** set up the **first formal anti-slavery groups**.
- **William Wilberforce**, an evangelical Christian and MP, led the campaign in Parliament.

| Year | Law |
|---|---|
| **1807** | **Abolition of the Slave Trade Act**: illegal to trade slaves in British ships or from British ports |
| **1833** | **Emancipation Act**: **slavery abolished** throughout the British Empire |

- After 1833, about **2 million** Indian and Chinese workers were brought in to replace freed slaves.

### American War of Independence

- The **13 American colonies** objected to paying taxes without representation in Parliament.
- **1776**: they declared **independence**.
- Britain recognised it in **1783**.

### War with France

| Year | Battle | Hero |
|---|---|---|
| **1805** | **Battle of Trafalgar** (at sea) | **Admiral Nelson**. He was killed. His ship **HMS Victory** is at **Portsmouth**. **Nelson's Column** stands in **Trafalgar Square**, London. |
| **1815** | **Battle of Waterloo** | The **Duke of Wellington** ("the Iron Duke") defeated **Napoleon**. He later became Prime Minister. |

### The Union of 1801

- **1801**: Ireland was united with Great Britain to form the **United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland**.
- The current **Union Flag** dates from this time.

⭐ **Must-know:** Arkwright · Bessemer · Watt · Sake Dean Mahomet (first curry house, 1810) · Wilberforce · 1807 & 1833 · 1776 · Trafalgar 1805 (Nelson) · Waterloo 1815 (Wellington) · 1801 Union with Ireland

## The Victorian age (1837–1901)

- **Queen Victoria** became queen in **1837**, aged **18**.
- She reigned for **64 years** (until 1901), then the longest reign in British history.

### The British Empire

- The **largest empire the world has ever seen**.
- Included **India, Australia** and large parts of **Africa**.
- Population of over **400 million** people.
- Between **1853 and 1913**, about **13 million** British citizens **emigrated**.
- People also came to Britain, e.g. **Russian Jews** (1880–1910) escaping violence (pogroms).

### Trade and industry

- **1846**: the **Corn Laws** were repealed. They had stopped cheap grain from being imported. This helped **free trade**.
- **1847**: working day for **women and children** limited to **10 hours**.
- **1851**: the **Great Exhibition** in **Hyde Park**, held in the **Crystal Palace**.

### Isambard Kingdom Brunel (1806–1859)

- Born in **Portsmouth**. Engineer.
- Built the **Great Western Railway** (first major railway in Britain), tunnels, bridges and ships.
- Famous works: **Clifton Suspension Bridge** (Bristol), **Paddington Station** (London).

### The Crimean War (1853–1856)

- Britain, with **Turkey and France**, fought **Russia**.
- The **Victoria Cross** medal was introduced to honour acts of bravery.

### Florence Nightingale (1820–1910)

- Born in **Italy** to English parents.
- Nursed soldiers in the **Crimean War**.
- **1860**: set up the **Nightingale Training School** for nurses at **St Thomas' Hospital**, London.
- Seen as the **founder of modern nursing**.

### Ireland

- **Potato famine** (mid-1800s): potato crops failed.
- Over **1 million** people died. About **1.5 million** emigrated (many to the USA).
- Growing demand for **"Home Rule"** (Ireland governing itself).

### The right to vote

| Year | Change |
|---|---|
| **1832** | **Reform Act**: more people could vote; abolished **"rotten boroughs"** (tiny places with an MP) |
| **1867** | **Second Reform Act**: more urban working men could vote |
| **1870 & 1882** | **Married Women's Property Acts**: wives could keep their own money and property |
| **1918** | Women **over 30** could vote |
| **1928** | Women could vote at **21**, the same age as men |
| **1969** | Voting age lowered to **18** for everyone |

- The **Chartists** campaigned for reform, including votes for all men and secret ballots.

### Emmeline Pankhurst (1858–1928)

- Born in **Manchester**.
- **1889**: helped form the **Women's Franchise League**.
- **1903**: helped found the **Women's Social and Political Union (WSPU)**.
- Its members were the **suffragettes**. They used civil disobedience, e.g. chaining themselves to railings, and some went on hunger strike.
- She died in **1928**, shortly before women got equal voting rights.

### The Boer War (1899–1902)

- Fought in **South Africa** against settlers of Dutch descent (the Boers).

### Rudyard Kipling (1865–1936)

- Born in **India**.
- Books: ***The Jungle Book***, ***Just So Stories***. Poem: ***"If"***.
- Won the **Nobel Prize in Literature** in **1907**.

⭐ **Must-know:** Victoria 1837–1901 (64 years) · Empire of 400 million · Brunel · Great Exhibition 1851 · Crimean War & Florence Nightingale · Irish famine · Reform Acts 1832/1867 · Pankhurst & suffragettes · 1918/1928 votes for women

## The First World War (1914–1918)

- Started after **Archduke Franz Ferdinand** of Austria was assassinated (**28 June 1914**).

| Allied Powers | Central Powers |
|---|---|
| Britain, France, Russia, Japan, Belgium, Serbia; later Greece, Italy, Romania and the **USA** (1917) | **Germany**, **Austria-Hungary**, the **Ottoman Empire**, **Bulgaria** |

- Over **1 million** soldiers from India fought for Britain; soldiers also came from the West Indies, Africa, Australia, New Zealand and Canada.
- **Battle of the Somme (July 1916)**: about **60,000** British casualties on the **first day**.
- The war ended at **11.00 am on 11 November 1918** (now Remembrance Day).

### The partition of Ireland

- **1913**: the government promised **Home Rule** for Ireland, but it was delayed by the war.
- **1916**: the **Easter Rising** in Dublin against the British. Leaders were executed.
- **1921**: peace treaty.
- **1922**: Ireland split into two:
  - **Northern Ireland** (6 northern, mainly Protestant counties) stayed in the UK.
  - The rest became the **Irish Free State**, which became a **republic in 1949**.
- Conflict continued in Northern Ireland. **"The Troubles"** began in the late **1960s**. Over **3,000** people died.

⭐ **Must-know:** 1914–1918 · Somme 1916 · 11 Nov 1918 · Easter Rising 1916 · 1922 partition · Republic 1949

---

## Between the wars

- **1920s**: living standards improved for many. Better public housing.
- **1929**: the **Great Depression**. Mass unemployment, especially in old industries (shipbuilding, coal).
- New industries grew: **cars** and **aviation**.
- **1922**: the **BBC** started radio broadcasts.
- **1936**: the BBC began the **world's first regular television service**.
- **1930s**: a house-building boom.

## The Second World War (1939–1945)

- **Adolf Hitler** led Germany. He invaded **Poland**.
- **3 September 1939**: Britain and France declared war on Germany.

| Axis Powers | Allies |
|---|---|
| **Germany, Italy, Japan** | **UK, France, Poland, Australia, New Zealand, Canada, South Africa**; later the **USA** and **USSR** |

### Key events

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1940** | Germany occupied **France**. |
| **May 1940** | **Winston Churchill** became Prime Minister. |
| **1940** | **Dunkirk**: many civilian volunteers in small boats helped rescue over **300,000** soldiers from France. Gave rise to the phrase **"the Dunkirk spirit"**. |
| **Summer 1940** | **Battle of Britain**: the **RAF** won air battles against Germany using **Spitfire** and **Hurricane** planes. |
| **1940–41** | **The Blitz**: German bombing of **London** and other cities like **Coventry**. |
| **Dec 1941** | **Japan** bombed **Pearl Harbor**. The **USA** joined the war. |
| **6 June 1944** | **D-Day**: Allied forces landed in **Normandy**, France. |
| **8 May 1945** | **VE Day**: Victory in Europe. |
| **Aug 1945** | **Japan surrendered** after atomic bombs were dropped on **Hiroshima** and **Nagasaki**. |

### Winston Churchill (1874–1965)

- Son of a politician. Soldier and journalist before politics.
- Conservative **MP from 1900**.
- **Prime Minister 1940–1945** and again **1951–1955**.
- Lost the 1945 election.
- Had a **state funeral** in 1965.
- Voted **greatest Briton of all time** by the public in **2002**.

| Famous Churchill quote | When |
|---|---|
| "I have nothing to offer but **blood, toil, tears and sweat**" | First speech as PM, May 1940 |
| "We shall **fight on the beaches**…" | After Dunkirk, June 1940 |
| "Never in the field of human conflict was so much owed by so many **to so few**" | About the RAF in the Battle of Britain, August 1940 |

### Science in the war

- **Alan Turing**: helped break German codes at **Bletchley Park**. Also invented the **Turing machine**, the basis of modern computers.
- **Alexander Fleming** (Scottish): discovered **penicillin** in **1928**. It was developed into a usable drug by **Howard Florey** and **Ernst Chain**. They shared a **Nobel Prize** (1945).

⭐ **Must-know:** 3 Sept 1939 · Churchill (PM May 1940) · Dunkirk · Battle of Britain · the Blitz · D-Day 6 June 1944 · VE Day 8 May 1945 · Turing · Fleming

## Post-war Britain

### The welfare state

- **1945**: the **Labour** Party won the election. **Clement Attlee** became PM.
- **1948**: the **National Health Service (NHS)** was created, free at the point of use.
  - Led by **Aneurin (Nye) Bevan**, the Minister for Health.
- **Nationalisation**: railways, coal mines, gas, water and electricity came under state control.
- Social security (benefits) for all.

| Person | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Clement Attlee (1883–1967)** | Labour PM **1945–1951**. Deputy PM to Churchill in wartime. Created the NHS and welfare state. Was a lawyer. |
| **William Beveridge (1879–1963)** | Liberal economist. The **Beveridge Report (1942)** "Social Insurance and Allied Services" was the basis of the welfare state. It named five **"Giant Evils"**: **Want, Disease, Ignorance, Squalor, Idleness**. |
| **R A Butler (1902–1982)** | Conservative. The **1944 Education Act** ("Butler Act") brought **free secondary education** in England and Wales. |
| **Dylan Thomas (1914–1953)** | Welsh poet and writer. *Under Milk Wood*; poem *"Do not go gentle into that good night"*. |

### End of empire

- **1947**: **India, Pakistan** and **Ceylon** (now Sri Lanka) became independent.
- Most other colonies became independent over the next 20 years.
- They joined the **Commonwealth**.
- Britain developed its own **atomic bomb** and joined **NATO**.

### Migration

- After the war Britain needed workers.
- People came from the **West Indies**, **India**, **Pakistan** and later **Bangladesh**.
- In the 1950s some British companies, e.g. bus companies, **recruited workers in the West Indies**.
- Late 1960s: immigration was restricted.
- **1970s**: Britain accepted **28,000** people of Indian origin forced out of **Uganda**, and about **22,000** refugees from **South East Asia** (e.g. Vietnam).

### The 1960s ("Swinging Sixties")

- Growth in **fashion, cinema and pop music**.
- Famous bands: **The Beatles** and **The Rolling Stones**.
- Laws on **divorce** and **abortion** were changed (England, Wales, Scotland).
- **Equal pay** and **anti-discrimination** laws followed.

⭐ **Must-know:** Attlee 1945 · NHS 1948 (Bevan) · Beveridge's 5 Giants · Butler Act 1944 · Independence of India 1947 · 1960s Beatles

## Britain since 1970

### Problems and Europe

- **1970s**: economic problems, **strikes** and industrial disputes.
- **1957**: the **Treaty of Rome** set up the European Economic Community (EEC). Britain **did not join** at first.
- **1973**: the UK **joined the EEC**.
- ⚠️ The UK **left the EU on 31 January 2020** ("Brexit").

### Margaret Thatcher (1925–2013)

- Born in **Grantham, Lincolnshire**. Father was a **grocer**. Trained as a **chemist** and **lawyer**.
- **Conservative**. **First woman Prime Minister** (1979–1990).
- **Longest-serving PM of the 20th century**.
- **Privatised** nationalised industries. Reduced trade union power.
- **1982**: the **Falklands War**. Argentina invaded the Falkland Islands; British forces recovered them.

### Later Prime Ministers

| PM | Party | Years | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **John Major** | Conservative | 1990–1997 | Helped start the **Northern Ireland peace process** |
| **Tony Blair** | Labour | 1997–2007 | Devolution to Scotland and Wales; Good Friday Agreement |
| **Gordon Brown** | Labour | 2007–2010 | |
| **David Cameron** | Conservative (coalition with Liberal Democrats) | 2010–2016 | First coalition since the war; **Nick Clegg** (Lib Dem) was Deputy PM |

⚠️ After the handbook: Theresa May (2016), Boris Johnson (2019), Liz Truss (2022), Rishi Sunak (2022), Keir Starmer (Labour, 2024).

### Devolution and peace

- **1998**: the **Good Friday Agreement** (Belfast Agreement) in Northern Ireland.
- **1999**: the **Scottish Parliament** and **Welsh Assembly** were created.
- **1999**: the **Northern Ireland Assembly** was elected (suspended 2002–2007).

### Wars

- UK forces fought in **Iraq** (from 2003) and **Afghanistan**.
- UK combat troops left Afghanistan in **2014**.

### Roald Dahl (1916–1990)

- Born in **Wales** to **Norwegian** parents.
- Served in the RAF in WWII.
- Children's books: ***Charlie and the Chocolate Factory***, ***George's Marvellous Medicine***.

⭐ **Must-know:** EEC 1973 · Thatcher first woman PM · Falklands 1982 · Good Friday Agreement 1998 · devolution 1999 · coalition 2010
